# Phase 1 — Competitor Killer Plan
**Project:** Yaya Mariana (premium artisanal fresas de Tarragona · ES/CA/EN)
**Prepared by:** competitor-killer
**Date:** 2026-05-20
**Mode:** AUDIT — surgical improvements inside the existing single-page section structure (Hero · StatsStrip · Products · AboutUs · Values · CTA · Contact · Footer · Cart). Deploy context: clean launch on fresh `yayamariana.es` TLD (NO domain migration, NO 301/410, NO SEO-equity preservation moves). Narrative is exclusively about la abuela Mariana; founder rendered as `"J. Elías, fundador"` — never expanded.

---

## 1. Executive summary

The Spanish premium-berry DTC category is a polished but emotionally cold landscape: **Crowdfarming** owns marketplace UX but dilutes the single-farm identity into a 300-farmer feed; **Naranjas del Carmen** is the corporate-clean single-producer template (the brand that grew INTO Crowdfarming) but hides the founder and leads with "how it works" instead of person or product; **Fruteva** and **Frutos Rojos Los Conventos** are interchangeable Spanish DTC produce templates without provenance depth; **Joselito** sets the aspirational ceiling for premium-artisanal Spanish food but is jamón, not fruit, and over-relies on a 150-year heritage that Yaya Mariana cannot fake. Across all five, **nobody owns the intersection of (a) variety-level craft storytelling, (b) named-grandmother emotional anchor, and (c) Tarragona / Catalunya micro-origin** — and that triple-intersection is exactly the white space the `clasico` build is already aimed at. The winning angle is therefore not invention, it's **amplification and restraint**: hold a 2-color Awwwards-tier discipline, surface the three varieties (Mágnum · Dream · 1525) as named craft objects, anchor the founder narrative on la abuela Mariana with first-person warmth, and let traceability microcopy (finca · harvest week · variety · Brix) close every product card. The three killers — Killer Feature: variety-named harvest-week chip in Hero; Killer Message: *"Las únicas fresas Mágnum, Dream y 1525 cultivadas con paciencia de abuela en el Camp de Tarragona"*; Killer Visual: la yaya Mariana's hands cradling a single Mágnum fresa over warm cream — all live inside the existing Hero with one image swap and a microcopy block.

## 3. Differentiation matrix (10 strengths × 5 weaknesses per competitor, concrete evidence)

**Strengths (10) — what they do that we must at least clear:**
1. Genuinely novel "adopt a tree/animal" interaction flow with visual clarity (no rival matches it).
2. Multilingual content depth across 6+ languages (ES/EN/DE/FR/IT/NL/PT), not just UI strings.

6. Mobile app extension of the e-commerce experience.
7. Same-day-harvest promise surfaced consistently across the site.

9. Strong structured data (Product + Farmer schemas visible in source).

**Weaknesses (5) — the cracks we exploit:**
1. Marketplace-corporate vibe (Etsy-for-farms) — *not* artisanal; emotional miss for a single-farm narrative.

3. "Notify me" workflow creates conversion friction for visitors who just want to buy today.
4. Adoption model confuses first-time visitors who don't want a relationship, they want a punnet.

## 4. Uncovered gaps + Winning angle

**Three uncovered gaps in the category** (confirmed against all 5 competitors and the Awwwards reference set):

1. **Variety-level storytelling** — No Spanish berry brand names its strawberry varieties as differentiated craft objects. Crowdfarming/Naranjas talk about farmers; Fruteva/Frutos Rojos talk about taste; nobody names *Mágnum · Dream · 1525* as the difference. Joselito proves variety/collection language works at the premium tier (his "vintage collections"). Yaya Mariana already has the three names in code — surfacing them as identity is a one-pass copy + badge move.
2. **Named-grandmother founder narrative** — Every direct competitor either hides the founder (Crowdfarming, Naranjas del Carmen, Fruteva) or names them weakly (Frutos Rojos). "La abuela Mariana" + `"J. Elías, fundador"` as a multi-generational craft duo is *unique* in the category. **HARD RULE: never expand the initials, never surface investor identity.** The narrative is la abuela cocinando con fresas — the founder is the grandson carrying the craft forward.
3. **Tarragona / Camp de Tarragona micro-origin** — 97% of Spanish strawberry production is Huelva. Catalonia is the second region; Maresme already commands a gifting premium for "fresones del Maresme". Tarragona is an even less-saturated micro-origin adjacent to recognized IGP Camp de Tarragona terroir vocabulary. No competitor in the sector owns it.

**Winning angle (one sentence):**
> *"Yaya Mariana is the only premium-DTC fresa brand that couples named-variety craft storytelling (Mágnum · Dream · 1525), a real Catalan-grandmother emotional anchor, and Tarragona micro-origin — held inside Awwwards-tier 2-color restraint, with traceability microcopy that out-trusts Crowdfarming on a single page."*

This positions Yaya Mariana **above** the Spanish berry mid-market (Fruteva/Frutos Rojos floor cleared decisively), **lateral to** Crowdfarming (different lane: warm-single-farm vs. clean-marketplace), and **on the same restraint axis as** Joselito (without claiming heritage we don't have).

### Killer Feature — *Variety + harvest-week traceability chip in Hero*
A single inline chip under the Hero headline rendering the live state: **"Recogidas en Tarragona · Mágnum · semana 18"** (auto-updates from a tiny data file or, in Phase 4.5, a CMS field). ### Killer Message — *one sentence no competitor can truthfully say*
> **"Las únicas fresas Mágnum, Dream y 1525 cultivadas con paciencia de abuela en el Camp de Tarragona."**

(CA: *"Les úniques maduixes Mágnum, Dream i 1525 cultivades amb paciència d'àvia al Camp de Tarragona."* · EN: *"The only Mágnum, Dream and 1525 strawberries grown with a grandmother's patience in the Camp de Tarragona."*)

Why no rival can copy it: Crowdfarming has 300 farmers (not "the only"); Naranjas del Carmen is naranjas; Fruteva and Frutos Rojos sell commodity-named berries (no Mágnum/Dream/1525 lexicon); Joselito is jamón. The sentence binds three uncopyable claims into one line that fits the existing Hero subtitle slot. **Founder rendered as `"J. Elías, fundador"`; abuela rendered as "Mariana" / "yaya Mariana". No initials-expansion anywhere in the rollout.**

### Killer Visual — *one image swap, existing Hero composition*
**La yaya Mariana's hands (warm, weathered, real — not stock) cradling a single Mágnum fresa over a warm-cream `#fdf6f5` ground, photographed close, shallow depth of field, no decorative props, no studio gloss.** Composed to occupy the existing Hero right-column 380×380 slot (replaces the current logo placement in that slot, OR sits as a secondary tile beneath the logo — exact placement TBD by hero-specialist + visual-perfection). The visual is Awwwards-restraint-aligned (Post Familiar Wine product-as-hero · Le Fruit Studio shallow-craft · Beerenberg Farm provenance) and uncopyable by any rival: Crowdfarming/Naranjas/Fruteva/Frutos Rojos all show flat-lay or field-wide photography, never a named grandmother's hands with a named variety. **Image-curator note: must be a real `yaya Mariana` portrait or a respectful close-up that does not surface investor identity — composition focuses on hands + berry, face out-of-frame or in soft focus.**

## 7. Counter-attacks — 3-5 surgical leapfrogs

These are the "what if a competitor responds?" preemptive moves. Each is surgical and stays inside the audit envelope.

1. **If Crowdfarming launches a single-variety "Tarragona partner" page** → we already own *named variety craft* (Mágnum/Dream/1525), Crowdfarming would have to onboard a Tarragona farm and onboard the varieties; we beat the timeline by 6+ months simply by amplifying Beat-them plan #1 (variety badges) and #8 (Hero typography statement) — they would be copying us, not leading.
2. **If Naranjas del Carmen adds a founder face** → we leapfrog by deepening Beat-them plan #4 (the multi-generational abuela + grandson craft duo) and adding a 30-60 second "yaya Mariana's hands" video loop in the existing AboutUs section (no new section). Naranjas would need to find a grandmother in their family — narrative defensibility favors us.
3. **If Fruteva/Frutos Rojos adopts variety names** → we counter via the *traceability chip* (Beat-them plan #2: "Recogidas en Tarragona · Mágnum · semana 18") + Brix degree in StatsStrip (Beat-them plan #3). They have neither the data discipline nor the single-finca provenance to match the chip's specificity; the moat is the *concrete data*, not the lexicon.
4. **If Joselito enters fruta** (low probability but high impact) → we lean into Catalan-language native UX (Beat-them plan #13 hreflang) and *Camp de Tarragona terroir* vocabulary in the AboutUs. Joselito is a national/international jamón brand; their reflex would be national copy, not Catalan-native copy. CA-native UX is a moat they would have to invest months to copy with credibility.
5. **If supermarket private label (Carrefour Bio / Eroski Eco) launches a "premium Catalan strawberry" SKU** → we lean on the named-variety + named-grandmother + finca-week chip *trio* simultaneously. Private label cannot fake a grandmother and will not name a single finca on a SKU. The trio is the structural moat against private-label encroachment — already the strongest defense the category has produced.
